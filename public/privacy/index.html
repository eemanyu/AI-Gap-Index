Privacy & data

Privacy policy.

Last updated: October 9, 2026. GAP Index is an independent editorial project. This page explains what the site collects and what happens when you sign up for email updates.

What the site collects

Reading the index does not require an account. The site is designed without advertising trackers or third-party analytics scripts. Standard hosting infrastructure may process technical request information, such as IP address, user agent, and server logs, according to the hosting provider's configuration.

Newsletter signups

When you subscribe, the server receives your email address and consent, normalizes the address, and records it with a timestamp in the site's SQLite database. If the site owner configures a Resend API key and audience ID, the address is also submitted to that mailing provider so it can be managed for future broadcasts. Without that configuration, the site stores the address locally and does not send newsletter emails.

News and external links

Recent news is fetched server-side from RSS search results and cached. Opening a linked source takes you to a third-party website governed by its own privacy policy. The site does not control those external services.

Retention and removal

Newsletter addresses remain on the local list until removed or until the site operator clears the database. Use the unsubscribe page to remove an address. If the mailing provider is configured, the same request marks the contact as unsubscribed in the provider before deleting the local record. Hosting backups and provider-side retention may follow separate retention schedules.

Data sharing and security

The site does not sell newsletter addresses. Access to the database depends on the server and hosting permissions configured by the operator. No online service can promise absolute security; avoid sending sensitive information through the signup form.

Changes

This policy may change as the site adds features or mailing services. The effective date above will be updated when material changes are made.
